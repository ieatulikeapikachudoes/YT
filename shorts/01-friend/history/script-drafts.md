# Friend short: how the script got here

## Draft 1 (rejected: "sounds like AI")
You are not going to make another close friend. Not because of you. Because of math.
Two hundred hours. That is what it takes to make a close friend. A researcher at Kansas counted. Fifty hours gets you a casual friend. Ninety gets you a friend. Two hundred gets you someone who shows up.
School gave you those hours for free. Same people, same room, every day, for years. You did not make friends. You were locked in a building with them.
Now you see people on purpose. Dinner, once a month, two hours. At that rate, a close friend takes eight years.
Nobody told you that. They let you think it was you.

## Draft 2 (rendered as v1)
You're not going to make another close friend. Hear me out.
A professor in Kansas actually counted. Fifty hours to get to casual. Around ninety and you'd call the person a friend. Two hundred before they're the one you call from the hospital.
Now think about where you got your friends. School. You didn't pick those people. Six hours a day, for years, whether you liked it or not. That's your two hundred hours. The building did it for you.
Then you graduate and suddenly friendship is an appointment. Dinner. Once a month, if everyone's free. Two hours, maybe. I did the math. It's the kind of thing I do. Eight years.
So no. It's not you. Nobody told you the building was doing the work.
