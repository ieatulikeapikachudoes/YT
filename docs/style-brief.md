# Style brief for the style agent

Read this, then the reference material Simon uploaded, then decide the look. Decide once per short and hold it across every scene. Simon gives notes on the output; fold each round of notes back into the Decided section below so choices narrow over time.

## Fixed (not yours to change)

- One character, one voice. Older man, suit, glasses, a KGB pin that is never mentioned or explained. No name.
- He talks straight, mostly deadpan, light humor thrown in. Bold claim first, then theory, studies, stories.
- The channel name does not appear in titles or on screen.
- Stick-figure animated explainer. Code-drawn (SVG, CSS), not AI-generated footage.
- Vertical 1080x1920 for shorts. Captions on screen, synced per line.
- Plain prose on screen. No em dashes anywhere.

## Open (yours to decide, using the references)

- Palette: paper and ink, or something else. Pick one accent color and use it for the one thing that matters per scene.
- What is around him: empty frame, a scene per beat, or diagrams. The references show three different answers.
- Type: face, weight, size of the claim card, size of captions, where captions sit.
- How a video opens: cold claim as big type, him walking in, or something recurring.
- How a video ends: a mirrored headline, him alone, a recurring line, or a cut.
- Motion vocabulary: how things enter (slide, pop, draw-on), how fast, how much he moves and gestures.
- Line quality of the character: stroke weight, whether he gets more detail (hands, shoes, hair) or less.

## How to decide

1. Look at the reference frames and name what each channel is doing: palette, density of the frame, type, how the figure is drawn, how much motion.
2. Pick one direction. Write it down in Decided before building anything.
3. Build the short in that direction. Every scene follows it.
4. Deliver the short plus a five-line summary of the choices, so Simon can react to the choices and not just the video.
