# Research: what if Trump strikes Iran after losing the midterms (Nov 4 2026)

## Where things stand
- The US and Israel have been at war with Iran since Feb 28 2026 (Operation Epic Fury). Ali Khamenei was killed on day one; Mojtaba Khamenei has been supreme leader since Mar 8. A ceasefire started Apr 8, then a Jun 17 memorandum, then it collapsed Jul 8. Since then it's "no war, no deal": a US naval blockade of Iranian ports, Hormuz mostly closed (8+ months), and small unannounced US strikes. [Wikipedia: 2026 Iran war; CGTN Nov 4]
- Nov 3 midterms: Democrats won the House and the Senate. Trump's approval was 37% on election day (NYT). 62% of voters said the war was the wrong decision; about 74% said it was a big factor in their vote (Responsible Statecraft exit poll, via ThePrint). Vance blamed the losses on the Iran war (Yahoo live blog headline).
- Before the vote, the WSJ reported Trump was leaning toward a large-scale attack after the midterms (via CGTN / MS NOW / Daily Beast). Inside the White House: Vance and Witkoff favor diplomacy; Netanyahu and Hegseth push for strikes; Rubio, Bessent and Ratcliffe favor the status quo (Axios via CGTN).
- Nov 4: Trump played it down. "We're locked and loaded if we need to go, but there may be no reason for it." "Maybe I will, maybe I won't." (Bloomberg via gCaptain; Fox live)
- The US hit Qeshm Island (in Hormuz) on Nov 2-3 after Iran targeted a US aircraft. Iran says it shot down 3 US drones. (WSJ via Iran International; Fox)
- Brent was $101.70 on Nov 4, up about 70% this year. (Bloomberg via gCaptain)
- Forces in the region: 3 carrier groups by late October (the Theodore Roosevelt is still on its way), about 20,000 sailors and Marines afloat, and nearly 40,000 US personnel at Gulf bases. (Soufan Center, Oct 8)

## The window
- The old Congress (Republican) sits until the new one is sworn in on Jan 3 2027. Analysts flag high escalation risk in this lame-duck window (Kuwait University's Hamad Althunayyan, via gCaptain).
- From January, Democrats plan to force a vote telling Trump to end the war or get authorization from Congress (NYT via Yahoo). A war-powers bill can still be vetoed, and overriding a veto takes 2/3 of both chambers.

## The conventional expected response to renewed strikes (Soufan Center chain, Oct 8)
1. US: renewed large-scale air and naval strikes, possibly plus limited ground raids to seize or destroy missile stocks and nuclear sites (not Trump's preferred option).
2. Iran: missile and drone salvos at US bases and Gulf energy infrastructure. Its doctrine is now counter-escalation: Ghalibaf said "the era of proportionate responses is over" and warned of a "nightmare."
3. Hormuz: the IRGC moves to close the southern lanes the US Navy now protects, and attacks on tankers resume.
4. Proxies: the Houthis (already hitting Saudi refineries, the East-West pipeline and airports) and Iraqi militias join in. If the Houthis threaten Aden, the US may have to open a Yemen front.
- In August, Iran threatened to hit the energy infrastructure of Gulf states that host US forces if strikes resume (Reuters/US News, Aug 5). This already happened in March: Ras Laffan (Qatar LNG), Ras Tanura (Saudi), Kuwait airport, Al Udeid.
- Capacity: US intelligence (May, NYT) says Iran kept about 70% of its prewar missiles, about 70% of mobile launchers, 30 of 33 Hormuz missile sites, and about 90% of underground missile bases. US officials aren't sure Iran can sustain a long campaign, or that US interceptor stocks would hold.
- Markets: Goldman, Fitch and WoodMac scenarios put Brent at $100-120 with a long closure, and WoodMac had $200 if the disruption lasts past September.
- Experts quoted by ThePrint: new strikes would more likely harden Tehran than force concessions.

- The premise has to be stated honestly: there's already a war, so this would be a *resumption at full scale*, not a first strike. Label the scenario HYPOTHETICAL like the Russia short.
- Map beats: Washington, then the carriers in the Gulf, then strike lines into Iran, then the counter-salvo arcs to Al Udeid, Ras Laffan and Ras Tanura, then Hormuz going dark, then the Houthis at Bab al-Mandab, then an oil ticker climbing, then a Jan 3 countdown.
